I'm Shreeya Jangid a postgraduate researcher and people strategist completing my MSc in Clinical Neuropsychiatry at the University of Birmingham. Originally from India, I moved to the UK driven by a deep fascination with how the brain shapes behaviour, performance, and wellbeing.

My academic work sits at the intersection of clinical neuroscience and real-world impact. I'm specifically focused on how neuropsychiatric conditions affect the way people think, work, lead, and recover — with my dissertation examining executive dysfunction after mild traumatic brain injury and its effects on emotional regulation and occupational functioning.

Beyond academia, I've built experience across HR operations, client experience, student representation, clinical shadowing, and community volunteering — across India and the UK. Fluent in English and Hindi, currently learning French. I'm drawn to high-stakes environments where precision and people both matter: luxury and motorsports are where I see my career heading.

I'm also an elected Student Representative for my MSc cohort and a Student Ambassador for the College of Medicine and Health — because I believe in advocating for the people around you, not just the research in front of you.

Ongoing · MSc Dissertation
Executive Dysfunction Following Mild Traumatic Brain Injury and Its Impact on Emotional Regulation and Occupational Functioning in Working-Age Adults
MSc Clinical Neuropsychiatry · University of Birmingham · Submission: August 2025

A systematic literature review examining how executive dysfunction arising from mild TBI cascades into disrupted emotional regulation and impaired occupational functioning. Synthesises neuropsychiatric and occupational health literature to surface clinical and workplace implications — bridging brain injury science with real-world recovery pathways for working adults.

Understanding Environmental Perspectives: Attitudes, Values and Self-Control
Associated with Manipal University Jaipur · Jan 2024 – Apr 2024

Explored the relationship between environmental attitudes, personal values, and self-control among BBA and BCom students. Mixed-methods approach using the Self-Control Scale (SCS), Environmental Values Questionnaire (EPVQ, 4 subscales), and Environmental Attitude Inventory (EAI, 12 subscales). Descriptive statistics, correlation, and t-test analyses revealed how these psychological factors interact in the context of environmental awareness and pro-environmental behaviour.
